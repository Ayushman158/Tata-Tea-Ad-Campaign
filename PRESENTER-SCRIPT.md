# Choose Assam: presenter script

Your own words, lightly tidied. About 3 minutes. Let the film play, then scroll the page as you talk.

---

**Film (let it play, sound on)**

**Overview**

"This project is for the module Designing Communication Ecologies. The brief was to take a social cause, tie it to a brand, and design a campaign system around it.

I took the recent floods in Assam. During the floods, a lot of people helped with food and money, and some even helped rebuild houses. But many small businesses were lost, and rebuilding a business is hard. Shops and workshops are only now starting to reopen, and people are trying to get their livelihood back.

So this campaign is about rebuilding. Tata Tea helps the makers, the small business owners of Assam, start their businesses again.

A lot of people still want to help. But with online donation drives, you don't know where the money is going, or whether it actually made a difference. So instead of donating, people buy from the makers, pre-order from them, or lend to them."

**Why Tata Tea**

"I chose Tata Tea because the tea in the pack comes from Assam, and the brand already speaks on social issues, like its Jaago Re campaign. It also owns the media: packs, shelves, billboards and ads that reach the whole country."

> Don't credit #KeralaIsOpen to Tata Tea. It was Samsonite India's campaign after the 2018 Kerala floods. Use it as a precedent: "#KeralaIsOpen brought tourists back after Kerala's floods. This does the same for Assam's makers."

**02 The makers**

"I chose six communities from Assam, each with its own craft: the tea-tribe pluckers, the Majuli mask makers, the Sarthebari bell-metal makers, who make utensils out of bell metal, the Mising weavers, the Bodo eri silk weavers and the jaapi makers. I generated each pattern with AI to represent one of these communities, and each one is based on that community's craft."

**03 What changed**

"This was the earlier packaging. It used the gamosa, which says Assam but doesn't credit anyone. This is the new packaging."

**04 Six editions**

"These are the six limited-edition packs for the campaign. The back of each pack has the community's story, where the craft is recorded, and a QR code."

**05 Out in the world**

"This is the billboard, and the gift box with all six packs and story cards."

**06 The platform**

"The QR code opens this website. People can buy from makers, pre-order, or lend money to these small businesses, so they become part of the system. Each maker has a status, Closed, Rebuilding, Producing or Open, and that decides what you can do. Each maker page also shows where the money goes, which is what donation drives don't do."

## Questions to prepare for

| Question | Short answer |
|---|---|
| Did you talk to any makers? | Not yet. This is desk research: flood reports, the GI registry and earlier campaigns. Talking to makers is the next step. |
| What did you design, and what did AI make? | I wrote the brief and the system, designed the pack system and built the website. The patterns, photos and film were generated with AI, and I directed them. |
| Hasn't Tata Tea done craft packs before? | Yes, Desh Ke Dhaage featured handloom weavers. Those packs credited a craft. These link to specific makers you can buy from. |
| How does lending work legally? | Through a licensed P2P lending partner. Equity crowdfunding isn't legal in India. The details are in the project notes. |
| Isn't using community crafts on a commercial pack a problem? | It can be. That's why each pack names the community and cites the craft, and a real edition would be made with them and paid for. |
