# Choose Assam: presenter script

About 4 minutes. Scroll the case study page as you talk. Say it in your own words; these are the points, not lines to read.

---

## 0:00 · Film (let it play about 20 seconds, sound on)

"This is Choose Assam, a campaign I designed for Tata Tea Premium. It's a speculative project for Designing Communication Ecologies."

## 0:30 · Overview

"Almost every monsoon, floods close workshops in Assam. The news covers it for a few days. Reopening takes months. Most flood campaigns ask for a donation, which helps once, and then the connection ends.

So I asked: what if the brand sent people to buy from the makers instead?

I wrote the brief and the system, designed the pack system, and built the maker platform. The photographs, patterns and film are generated with AI, and I art-directed them. Nothing here has been tested with makers or buyers yet."

> Say the AI part early and plainly. A juror will ask, so it's better to say it before they do.

## 0:50 · 01 After the water, and what already exists

"The idea: buy from makers instead of donating.

Parts of this already exist. Tata Tea's own Desh Ke Dhaage packs featured handloom weavers. #KeralaIsOpen brought visitors back after Kerala's 2018 floods. Chekutty dolls were made from flood-ruined saris. None of them connects a pack to a specific maker. That's the gap I designed for."

## 1:20 · 02 The makers

"The campaign starts with six communities and their crafts: tea-tribe pluckers, Mising weavers, Bodo eri silk, Majuli mask makers, Sarthebari bell metal and jaapi makers. Every pattern on the pack comes from one craft. Five of them are recorded as Geographical Indications, and the tea-tribe one draws on Jhumur."

## 1:40 · 03 What changed

"My first pack used the gamosa. It said Assam, but not whose work it was. The gamosa is on everything from Assam, so it didn't credit anyone. Each edition now names one community on the front and uses a pattern from its craft."

> This is the slide that shows iteration. Pause here.

## 2:00 · 04 Six editions

"The Tata Tea Premium label stays the same; only the pattern changes. The back tells the community's story, names where the craft is recorded, and has a QR to the makers who are open this week."

## 2:20 · 05 Out in the world

"Every surface carries the same QR: billboards, the pack, a gift box with story cards."

## 2:40 · 06 The platform

"The QR lands here. 24 makers, filterable by craft, status and district, with a map. Each maker has a status: Closed, Rebuilding, Producing or Open. The status decides what you can do. You can lend to a maker who is rebuilding, pre-order once they're producing, and buy once they're open. That way nobody pays for something that can't ship."

> Open the live platform here if you have a screen to click on.

## 3:10 · Tradeoffs

"Three decisions and what they cost. No donate button: harder to explain, and it only helps makers who can sell again. One QR on every pack, with everything that changes kept on the platform: it only works if someone keeps the statuses up to date. And using community crafts on a commercial pack means the patterns would have to be made with those communities and paid for before anything is printed."

## 3:40 · Close

"What's still open: real makers, patterns drawn with each community, and checking the Majuli masks against real satra masks. I'd measure success by repeat orders per maker, and how many makers reach Open within 90 days."

## Questions to prepare for

| Question | Short answer |
|---|---|
| Did you talk to any makers? | No. This is desk research: flood reports, the GI registry and past campaigns. Talking to makers is the next step. |
| Isn't this just Desh Ke Dhaage again? | That credited a craft. This links each pack to living makers, through a platform with status and ledgers. |
| What did you make, and what did AI make? | I made the brief, the system, the pack system, the copy and the platform build. AI made the imagery and the film, which I art-directed and corrected. |
| Why would Tata do this? | Assam is in the product, the brand already speaks on public issues (Jaago Re), and it owns the media. |
| How does lending work legally? | Through a licensed P2P partner. Equity crowdfunding isn't legal in India. The details are in the project notes. |
| Isn't using tribal imagery on a commercial pack a problem? | Yes, it can be. That's why each community is named and its craft is cited, and why a real edition must be co-made and paid for. |
